# FinanceBook

**A simple, private bookkeeping app for your own money — right on your Windows PC.**

FinanceBook helps you keep the books for your household, rental properties, or a
small business without a monthly subscription, without an online account, and
without handing your finances to anyone else. Everything lives in a single file
on your own computer.

Think of it as a lightweight alternative to QuickBooks: track several accounts,
import your bank and credit-card activity, sort transactions into categories,
reconcile against your statements, and print clean Profit & Loss and Balance
Sheet reports.

## New: pack sizes on receipts (v1.11)

> **This release upgrades the database format again.** Update FinanceBook on every
> computer that shares a database (e.g. through OneDrive) before opening it there.

- **Units per pack on every receipt line.** A "6 Pack" bought once is 6 units. Each
  line in Review Receipt now has **Units per pack** (editable, whole number, 1 or
  more), **Total units** (qty × units per pack) and **Cost per unit** — for Inventory
  lines that's the landed cost (the line plus its share of tax, tip, shipping and
  duties, less discount). They update as you type, are saved with drafts, and can
  be corrected after a receipt is booked (**Save Changes** updates the stock it
  received; the booked amount doesn't change).
- **Suggested from the title.** "6 Pack", "Pack of 6", "6-Pack", "Set of 4",
  "12-count" and "12 ct" fill in units per pack (highlighted — check it).
  Ambiguous titles like "3 Pack of 10 ft cables" are pointed out but not applied.
- **Stock in units.** Inventory lines add qty × units per pack to stock at the
  per-unit landed cost (kept to 6 decimals so the units add back up to the exact
  cents). Stock items have a default **Units per pack** that pre-fills receipt
  lines; change it on a line and FinanceBook offers to make it the new default.
- **Fix:** receipts split into several lines booked Inventory, Fixed asset and
  uncategorized lines as money *in*. They're now booked as money out. Receipts
  booked with v1.10.1 or earlier aren't changed — re-save affected ones.

## New: better receipt reading, shipping & inventory (v1.10)

> **This release upgrades the database format.** If you share a database
> between computers (for example through OneDrive), update FinanceBook on
> **every** computer before opening it there — an older FinanceBook refuses to
> open a database a newer one has upgraded. A backup is made before the upgrade.

- **Many more PDFs read on their own.** FinanceBook now reads PDFs the way a PDF
  viewer lays them out, so table rows (item, qty, price) stay on one line:
  - pages printed from Chrome or Edge ("Save as PDF" of an order page — e.g. an
    **Amazon Order Details** page), which use embedded Unicode fonts;
  - invoices generated by business systems (e.g. **DigiKey**), including
    multi-page ones;
  - PDFs saved as "PDF 1.5" with compressed object streams.
- **Image-only PDFs and photos are read offline** with the text recognition built
  into Windows 10/11 (nothing to install, nothing leaves your PC). A PDF that is
  just a picture of an invoice (e.g. a **JLCPCB** invoice) is recognised and read
  this way. If Windows has no OCR language installed, you'll see "This is an
  image. Use Read with AI, or type it in."
- **Vendor-aware reading.** Amazon (order date, order #, multi-line item titles,
  several items and shipments, Qty, free-shipping credits, coupons and gift
  cards), DigiKey (part numbers, invoice #, "Sales Amount" vs. the
  after-shipping "Charges subtotal") and JLCPCB (merchandise total, discount,
  import taxes, day/month dates) are handled specifically; everything else uses
  the general reader, which also got smarter.
- **Shipping, discounts and import duties.** Receipts now have **Shipping /
  handling**, **Discount** and **Import duties / fees** next to Tax and Tip, and
  the check is *items + shipping + duties + tax + tip − discount = total*. The
  subtotal is always the items only. Spread them over the items, or book tax,
  tip, shipping and duties as their own lines (business books default shipping to
  the **Shipping** category).
- **Ambiguous dates are flagged.** A date like 08/10/2026 could be August or
  October; FinanceBook uses what it knows about the vendor (JLCPCB writes
  day/month) and otherwise shows "Date is ambiguous — check it".
- **Pick the inventory item on a receipt line.** Set a line's type to
  **Inventory** (or choose the Inventory Purchases category) and pick the stock
  item — or **➕ New item…**, prefilled with the description and the part number
  (e.g. DigiKey's PART). Stock is received at its **landed cost** (its share of
  shipping, duties and tax, less discount), and saving a receipt again replaces
  that stock instead of adding it twice. Fixed assets also use the landed cost.
- **Cleaner business categories.** A personal database turned into a business
  book keeps all its personal categories (nothing is deleted, reports still show
  everything), but category lists now show the business (Schedule C) categories.
  Personal ones you've used stay visible; tick **Show personal categories** on
  the Categories page to see the rest. Personal receipt lines (Business unticked)
  can still use any category.

## New: one database on two computers (v1.9)

Keep your database in a **OneDrive** (or Google Drive / network) folder and use it
from more than one computer — for example a desktop and a laptop.

- **Connect Existing Database…** — on the second computer, choose this on the
  welcome screen (or later from the sidebar's database list, or **Settings →
  Database**) and pick the `.db` file in your OneDrive folder. FinanceBook checks
  the file first without changing it: it refuses a file that's still downloading
  or "online-only", a file that isn't a FinanceBook database, or one already
  upgraded by a newer FinanceBook (update that computer first). It warns you about
  OneDrive conflict copies (like `finances-DESKTOP-AB12CD.db`).
- **A lock stops accidental double use.** While a database is open, FinanceBook
  keeps a small `finances.db.lock` file next to it. If you open the same database
  on the other computer, it tells you where it's in use and lets you **Open
  Read-Only**, **Take Over** (only if the other computer crashed or is off), or
  cancel. Read-only really is read-only — nothing can be saved, and a yellow
  banner says so.
- **Safer syncing.** The database file is now complete on disk after every save
  (no separate `-wal` / `-shm` files for OneDrive to sync out of step), and
  FinanceBook won't quietly create an empty database if your OneDrive folder
  isn't available yet — it lets you retry or choose another database.

**Using two laptops with OneDrive**

1. On laptop A, create (or keep) your database in a folder inside OneDrive.
2. When you're done on laptop A, **close FinanceBook** and let OneDrive finish
   syncing (the OneDrive icon shows "Up to date").
3. On laptop B, make sure the folder is synced too. Right-click the folder →
   **Always keep on this device** is recommended.
4. Start FinanceBook on laptop B → **Connect Existing Database…** → pick the same
   `.db` file. From then on, laptop B opens it automatically.
5. Switch back and forth the same way: close on one, wait for sync, open on the other.

**Honest limitation:** the lock file travels through OneDrive like any other file,
so the other computer only sees it once OneDrive has synced it. It reliably
catches a second copy of FinanceBook on the *same* computer, and the other
computer once it's in sync — but it can't stop two computers that are offline
or out of sync from both editing. Always let OneDrive finish syncing before
opening the database on the other computer. If OneDrive ever shows a conflict
copy, keep both files until you've checked which one has your latest changes.
Each computer's own settings (which databases it knows about) stay on that
computer and are never put in the synced folder.

## New: business books (v1.1)

FinanceBook can now keep the books for a **small business** (sole proprietor or
single-member LLC), not just personal money. Create a new database and choose
**Business**, or switch an existing one in **Settings → Book Type**.

- **No business bank account yet? No problem.** Record company expenses you
  pay out of your own pocket as either a **capital contribution** (your
  investment) or money the **company owes you**. Pay yourself back later with
  one click (**Reimburse Owner**), and switch to a real business account
  whenever you open one.
- **Owner Investment page** — how much you've put in, been repaid, taken out,
  and are still owed, with every owner-paid expense listed and a CSV export.
- **Balance Sheet with an Equity section** — owner contributions, draws,
  retained earnings and this year's profit, and it always balances.
- **Schedule C categories** — business categories mapped to IRS Schedule C
  lines (meals flagged 50%), including a full Advertising & Marketing group.
- **Automatic backups** — a daily backup next to your database (keeps the last
  10), a backup before every upgrade, and one-click backup/restore in Settings.

- **Receipts (v1.2)** — drag in photos, PDFs, emailed receipts (.eml), saved web
  pages or text. FinanceBook reads the vendor, date, tax, tip and line items,
  checks they add up, and lets you pick a category, project and business/personal
  for each item before saving it as one split transaction with the receipt
  attached. It remembers each vendor's usual category, and you can search every
  item you've ever bought ("what did I spend on printer paper?").
- **Optional AI receipt reading** — for photos and scanned PDFs, paste your own
  Anthropic API key in Settings and click **Read with AI**. Off by default; the
  receipt file is only sent when you click.
- **Attach receipts to any transaction** — including imported bank lines (look
  for the 📎), then itemize that bank charge from its receipt.
- **Fixed assets & depreciation (v1.3)** — keep a register of machines,
  vehicles, equipment and prepaid expenses (insurance, licenses). FinanceBook
  calculates depreciation automatically — straight-line, declining balance,
  units of production (machine hours), Section 179 / bonus, or MACRS — shows it
  on the P&L, shows assets at book value on the Balance Sheet, and works out the
  gain or loss when you sell one. Add an asset straight from a receipt line or
  a transaction.
- **Invoices, bills & contacts (v1.4)** — keep vendors and customers (with
  payment terms and 1099 flags), send numbered invoices as PDFs, enter bills,
  and record payments (each becomes a transaction automatically; sales tax on
  invoices goes to a Sales Tax Payable liability). A/R and A/P aging show
  what's overdue, the dashboard shows what you're owed and what you owe, and
  imported bank lines are matched to vendors automatically.
- **Inventory (v1.5)** — items with SKUs and reorder points, stock received
  and sold, stock counts, and FIFO or weighted-average costing. Stock you buy
  sits on the Balance Sheet as Inventory and moves to Cost of Goods Sold on the
  P&L when it sells. Low-stock items are flagged; a valuation report exports to
  CSV. Add stock straight from a receipt line.
- **Employees, payroll & 1099s (v1.6)** — W-2 employees with W-4 details,
  hours logging, and payroll runs that **estimate** federal withholding (IRS
  Pub 15-T 2026), Social Security and Medicare, FUTA/SUTA and state withholding,
  then book wages, net pay and the taxes you owe (shown on the Balance Sheet
  until you pay them). The Contractors tab totals payments to each 1099
  contractor and flags who crosses the 1099-NEC threshold ($2,000 for payments
  made in 2026), with a CSV summary. Payroll figures are estimates — not a
  substitute for a payroll provider or accountant.
- **Taxes & reports (v1.7)** — a Schedule C summary grouped by tax line (meals
  at 50%), quarterly estimated-tax estimate with 1040-ES due dates, sales tax
  collected vs. paid, a mileage log at the IRS rate for each trip's date, a
  home-office calculator (simplified vs. actual), and a one-click **Year-End
  Package** of CSVs for your accountant. New report tabs: P&L by month, by
  class / project / campaign (with ROI), by vendor, by person, cash flow
  statement, and budget vs. actual.
- **Recurring, audit trail & period lock (v1.8)** — set up rent, subscriptions
  and other repeats to post automatically or remind you; every change is logged
  with who made it and when (Settings → View Audit Log); lock a month or year
  once it's closed so nothing in it changes by accident. Business books get a
  new dashboard: cash on hand, revenue / expenses / profit this year, what
  you're owed and owe, what you've invested, and a "Coming up" list.

Tax-related labels are bookkeeping aids, not tax advice — confirm treatment with
your accountant.

## What you can do with it

- **Track all your accounts in one place** — checking, savings, credit cards,
  loans, and more, each with its own running balance.
- **Import from your bank** — drop in the files your bank exports
  (`.qfx` / `.qbo`, `.ofx`, `.csv`, `.xlsx`, or QuickBooks `.iif`) and
  FinanceBook adds the transactions for you, skipping duplicates.
- **Organize spending** — assign transactions to categories and subcategories so
  you can see where the money actually goes.
- **Automate the busywork** — set up rules that categorize recurring
  transactions automatically as they come in, and recurring items (rent,
  subscriptions) that post themselves or remind you.
- **Keep an audit trail** — see who changed what and when, and lock closed
  periods.
- **Reconcile against statements** — tick off transactions and confirm your
  records match the bank, the same way an accountant would.
- **See the whole picture** — Profit & Loss, Balance Sheet, cash flow, P&L by
  month / class / vendor, and budget vs. actual — all exportable — plus a
  dashboard summarizing your accounts.
- **Track loans** — record a loan and FinanceBook works out the payment
  schedule, splitting each payment into principal and interest.
- **Keep separate books** — run completely separate databases for, say,
  personal, company, and property finances, and switch between them. Each one
  is either a **Personal** or a **Business** book.
- **Run a small business** — track money you put in or take out as the owner
  (even with no business bank account), see a proper Balance Sheet with an
  Equity section, and use categories mapped to Schedule C.
- **Stay backed up** — automatic daily backups next to each database, plus
  one-click backup and restore.
- **Run simple payroll** — estimated withholding and payroll taxes, plus 1099 tracking.
- **Track inventory** — stock on hand, FIFO / average cost, and cost of goods sold.
- **Invoice customers and track bills** — numbered PDF invoices, bills,
  payments, and A/R / A/P aging.
- **Track equipment and depreciation** — an asset register with automatic
  depreciation schedules and gain/loss on sale.
- **Keep receipts with your books** — attach receipt files to transactions and
  turn itemized receipts into split transactions automatically.

## Your data stays yours

FinanceBook runs entirely on your computer. There is **no cloud account, no sign
up, and no internet connection required** to use it. Your finances are saved in a
local database file that only you can see — you even choose where each database
is stored. The only thing that ever goes online is an optional check for app
updates.

## Getting started

1. Download the latest **`FinanceBook-x.y.z.zip`** from the
   [Releases page](https://github.com/Adeen924/FinanceBook/releases/latest).
2. Extract it anywhere you like (for example, your Desktop or Documents folder).
3. Double-click **`FinanceBook.exe`**. There's nothing to install — everything
   the app needs is bundled inside.

The first time you open it, a short guided tour walks you through setting up your
first account and importing transactions.

## Staying up to date

When a new version is available, FinanceBook shows an **"Update available"**
banner when you start it. Click **Install Update** once — the app downloads the
update, restarts, and reopens on the new version. **Your data is never touched**
during an update; it lives separately from the app itself.
